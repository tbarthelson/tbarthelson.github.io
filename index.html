When to Go?

Tasmania lies in the path of the "Roaring Forties" winds. The hiking window is narrow. December through March is critical for alpine access.

  • ☀ Best Bet: February offers the warmest days and lowest average rainfall.
  • ❄ Warning: Snow can fall on Cradle Mountain even in summer. Pack for all seasons.

East Coast vs. West Coast

Tasmania is a tale of two climates. The West is rugged, alpine, and wet (ancient rainforests). The East is drier, coastal, and relaxed (white sands and granite).

West (Cradle) Dramatic peaks, wombats, rain gear essential.
East (Freycinet) Swimming, sunrise views, coastal breezes.

The 14-Day "Loop" Itinerary

Days 1-3 • The South

Hobart & Wilderness

Fly into Hobart. Pick up rental car (AWD recommended).

  • 📍 Mt Wellington: Drive to summit, hike "Organ Pipes".
  • 📍 Bruny Island: Ferry across for the Fluted Cape Walk.
  • 📍 Tasman Peninsula: Begin the Three Capes Track or day hike to Cape Raoul.
Days 4-7 • The East Coast

Sun & Granite

Drive North along the coast.

  • 📍 Freycinet NP: Sunrise hike up Mt Amos (Steep/Slippery).
  • 📍 Wineglass Bay: Beach walk and swim.
  • 📍 Bay of Fires: Iconic orange rocks. easy coastal walking.
Days 8-11 • The Alpine Heart

Cradle Mountain

Drive inland to the high country. Stay at Cradle Valley.

  • 📍 The Summit: 8hr return, scrambling required.
  • 📍 Dove Lake Circuit: 2hr easier scenic walk.
  • 📍 Wombat Spotting: Ronny Creek at dusk.
Days 12-14 • The Return

Waterfalls & Departure

Loop back towards Hobart via the center.

  • 📍 Mt Field NP: Russell Falls & Tarn Shelf (Alpine lakes).
  • 📍 Return: Hobart for a final seafood dinner. Fly out.
